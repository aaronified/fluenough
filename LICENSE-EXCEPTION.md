## Why this exception exists

The GNU GPL version 3 forbids imposing "further restrictions" on the rights it
grants (sections 7 and 10). The terms of service of some application
distribution services — most notably Apple's App Store — do impose such
restrictions, including limits on the number of devices a copy may be
installed on, and digital rights management applied to the delivered binary.

Without this exception, Fluenough could not be distributed through the Apple
App Store at all. This is not a theoretical concern: it is why GNU Go was
removed from the App Store in 2010, and why the VLC project had to relicense
in order to ship on iOS.

This exception resolves that conflict **narrowly**, and only for the act of
distribution.

## What this exception does NOT do

This exception does not weaken the copyleft of Fluenough in any way. In
particular, it does **not** permit anyone to:

- distribute Fluenough, or any work based on it, without also making the
  Corresponding Source available under the GPL;
- relicense Fluenough, or any work based on it, under any licence other than
  the GPL with this same exception;
- create a proprietary or closed-source fork; or
- depart from the terms of the GPL in any other respect.

Anyone distributing Fluenough through an app store under this exception must
ensure that the Corresponding Source for that exact version remains publicly
available, at no charge, through a channel that carries no such restrictions.

## Contributions

Because this exception is part of the project licence from the first commit,
all contributions are made under "GPL-3.0 with the Fluenough App Store
Distribution Exception". **No contributor licence agreement is required.**

## Extending the exception

If you modify Fluenough, you may extend this exception to your version, but
you are not obliged to do so. If you do not wish to extend it, delete this
statement from your version.

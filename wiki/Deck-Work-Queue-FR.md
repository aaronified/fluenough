- Before a builder writes any word card it lists the lemmas the course already has (every `target` in `decks/fr/*.yaml`, on the shared branch and `decks-live`), and uses `ref` for a word that exists.

## 3. French conventions every FR task carries

### 3.1 Conventions of the existing French decks (read from `fr-travel`, `fr-clothes-sentences`, `fr-grammar-etre`, `fr-en-reading-futur-proche`)

- **Header of a core**: `schema: 1`, `id: "fr-<name>"`, `part: "core"`, `kind: "vocab"` (or `"grammar"`), `language: { code: "fr", iso639_3: "fra", name: "French", script: "latin", tts: "fr-FR", icon: "Fr" }`, `license: "CC-BY-SA-4.0"`, `theme: "<theme>"` (a word unit; the ten planned themes are already in `decks/themes.yaml`), `tags: ["beginner", "unreviewed"]` (a grammar core: `["beginner", "grammar", "unreviewed"]`), then `cards:`. Header of a layer: `schema: 1`, `id: "fr-en-<name>"`, `kind: "layer"`, `core: "fr-<name>"`, `native: { code: "en", iso639_3: "eng", name: "English" }`, `name`, `license`, `description` ending "Not yet checked by a French speaker: please report any mistake.", then `cards:` keyed by card id. Header of a reading deck: as `fr-en-reading-futur-proche` (`kind: "reading"`, `native`, `source: "Written for this deck"`, `tags: ["reading", "unreviewed"]`, `passages:`).
- **Readings.** French is written in Latin letters: **no `reading` field** anywhere (no `NO_READING` problem: script `latin`). **`ipa` is written by the builder** (the tool for it is FR-120, section 6 D1) in the broad notation of the existing French cards: no stress mark and no length mark; `ʁ` for r; the nasal vowels `ɛ̃ ɑ̃ ɔ̃ œ̃`; `ɥ`, `j`, `w` for the semivowels; the schwa `ə` written; silent final letters not written; a space between words; an elided clitic joined to the next word (`l'arrivée` is `laʁive`); a liaison consonant written where it is said (`lez ami`, `dø zɑ̃`), as in the nearest existing card. A word card's `ipa` is read from the word's en.wiktionary French "Pronunciation" section and put into these conventions; a word whose page cannot be read (after the polite retries of section 3.2) is dropped from the unit and reported, never guessed. A sentence's `ipa` joins its words' forms with the elision and liaison of the existing sentence decks. **No `phonetic` and no `phonetic_source` on any new card** (FR-123 writes them; narrow IPA is never written by hand). A card without a broad `ipa` is an error unless it is a `kind: "set"` card.
- **Ids.** `fr-NNNN`, four digits, from the task's range, ascending; `--next-id` is not used (ranges are reserved). Confusable ids and note ids are `[a-z0-9-]+`, ASCII only, no accents.
- **Word card** (shape of `fr-travel`): `id`, `target` (the dictionary form: infinitive for a verb, masculine singular for an adjective, singular without article for a noun), `ipa`, `pos` (`noun`, `verb`, `adjective`, `adverb`, ...), `gender: "m"` or `"f"` on every noun (never on other words; the path's `genders` are m and f), `notes: [{ id: "n1", kind: "note" }]`, `bases: "self"` (the word is its own root), `examples` (exactly 2): each `{ target, ipa, bases: [...] }`.
- **`bases` of a target or an example** (Deck-Format "Base words"): one entry for every word of the text, in order. `{ word: "<word as written>", ref: "<card id>" }` when the word, as written (ignoring a capital), is exactly the `target` of an existing course card; `{ word: "<word as written>", base: "<its dictionary form>" }` for a word with no card, for an inflected, elided or contracted form (`vais` base `aller`, `au` base `à`, `l'arrivée` base `arrivée`), the same shape as the existing decks. A word of the text is a word of the target as written; apostrophe and hyphen words are one word (`s'il`, `peut-être`).
- **Layer of a core**: `cards:` keyed by id: `native` (ONE sentence or phrase, joining clauses with `;`), `notes` keyed by the core note's id (`"n1": "..."`; plain English prose), `examples` keyed by the example's target: `{ native, bases: { "<word>": "<English gloss>" } }` where the gloss list holds **only the inline (`base`) words** of that example, one per word (so it is empty, and the `bases` key is left out entirely, when every word is a `ref`; never write `bases:` empty).
- **Sets.** A closed group a unit teaches (days, months, colours, numbers, pronouns, articles, question words) gets one `kind: "set"` card with `members` by `ref` and its name and explanation in the layer (Deck-Format "Set cards"). A set card has no `ipa`.
- **Sentence cards** (shape of `fr-clothes-sentences`): `id`, `target`, `ipa`, `pos: "phrase"`, `tags: ["sentence"]`, `bases` (as above), and `confusables`: 2 to 3 entries `{ id: "<ascii-slug>", ref: "<id of an existing word card>", kind: "word" }`, none a word of the sentence. The layer gives `native` (the full translation, one sentence), the confusables' `why` keyed by the confusable `id` (`"manteau": { why: "Manteau is 'coat', ..." }`), and inline word glosses. **Sentence gate**: a whole sentence ending in `.`, `?` or `!`; 4 to 9 words; every word is a card of a deck listed on the path at or before the unit, or of the unit's own word deck, or has an inline base (no new vocabulary is invented to fit a sentence); the target and the meaning are unique in the course (the builder searches `decks/fr` first); no two sentences of a deck share a skeleton or differ by one word; at least 3 sentence shapes and about 2 questions in 10; no content noun more than twice; a verb no more than twice; shuffled so no run of three has one shape. Ten sentences per unit (eight if the unit has fewer than 20 words); never padded.
- **Genre decks** (culture, proverbs, multi-word) add: `genre`, `level` in the header; on each card `words` (the entries joined with single spaces give the target with its punctuation marks removed; no entry contains a space; apostrophe and hyphen words are one entry), `level`, `modes`, and the genre fields of sections 4.4. Layers of genre decks gloss each inline word as above.
- **Notes**: `notes` in a core is a list of `{ id, kind }` (and `ref` on `pair`, `homograph`, `lookalike`; `source` on `culture`); the text lives in the layer by that id.

### 3.3 Recipe RULE-WORD-FR (the 10 word units, FR-010 to FR-019)

For each word unit, one worker, in its own worktree off the shared branch, writes **all** of these in one task; paths use the unit's theme `T` (for `fr-illness`, `T` is `illness`):

1. `decks/fr/fr-T.yaml` and `decks/fr/en/fr-en-T.yaml`: the planned number of word cards (the planned `words`, 10% under allowed, never over by more than 5), ids ascending from the task's range, shape of section 3.1. Scope is given in the task row; before choosing words the worker lists the lemmas of `decks/fr` and leaves out every lemma that is already a card (a needed one is a `ref`). Choose the commonest, most useful words of the scope (check frequency on fr.wiktionary or a cited word list it read); no rare words, no regional words, no vulgar words. Every word card has 2 examples that use only taught words (cards of decks listed on the path before the unit, plus the unit's own words) or inline bases, a note `n1` (kind `note`, one teachable fact: the gender of a noun, a feminine form, a stem change, a false friend), and every noun a gender. A closed group inside the scope gets a set card.
2. `decks/fr/fr-T-sentences.yaml` and `decks/fr/en/fr-en-T-sentences.yaml`: 10 sentence cards (section 3.1), ids following the word cards, each practising words of the unit (every sentence uses at least one word of the unit).
3. `decks/fr/fr-en-reading-T.yaml`: the reading deck of the unit (section 3.5), ids following the sentences.
4. `decks/fr/fr-path.yaml`: edit **only this unit's entry**: `planned` becomes `decks: ["fr-T", "fr-T-sentences", "fr-reading-T", "*"]`, `words:` the planned number or the count if higher, `grammar:` kept if present, `milestone` kept where present (unit 72 `"A2"`), and both passage lists kept exactly. 5. Not touched: every file not named, `decks/min_app.json`, `decks/themes.yaml` (the theme exists), `decks/index.json`, `docs/review-queue/fr.md` (the report carries the rows, rule 8).

### 3.4 Recipe RULE-GRAMMAR-FR (the 25 grammar units; Sonnet)

A grammar unit teaches a pattern over words the course already teaches (build-deck skill, "Grammar units"). **Design is Sonnet's work**: the slots (permanent in order), the entries and the forms.

1. **Deck names are the topics.** Core `decks/fr/fr-grammar-<topic>.yaml`, layer `decks/fr/en/fr-en-grammar-<topic>.yaml`, one pair for each topic in the unit's `grammar` list (the planned id is not used unless it equals the topic: `fr-grammar-future` teaches the topic `futur-simple`, so the deck is `fr-grammar-futur-simple`). The validator takes a topic as "taught by a listed `fr-grammar-<topic>` deck or a rule"; a unit with two topics has two cores.
2. **Kind.** `kind: "grammar"` (a pattern over a chosen set), as all 23 existing French grammar cores are; header `tags: ["beginner", "grammar", "unreviewed"]`; `pattern: { slots: [...], entries: [...] }`, each entry a `lemma`, a `key` (`[a-z0-9-]+`, ASCII, when the lemma has accents or spaces), `ipa`, and `forms` with a form for every slot (`null` for none; a cell with two right forms lists both); no `gloss`, `name`, `slot_name` or `prompt` in the core. Slot keys are quoted. Entries: 6 to 12.
3. **Words already taught only.** Every lemma and every form uses words that are cards of decks listed on the path before the unit (look each up by `target` in `decks/fr`). **A word of a still-planned unit is off limits** unless that unit's deck is already on the shared branch. A missing word means the unit is planned too early: stop and report.
4. **Layer**: `pattern` with `name`, `slot_name`, `prompt` (`"{lemma} ({gloss}), {slot}"`), `slots` (a label for each slot), `entries` (a gloss for **every** entry, keyed by `key` or lemma), and `notes` (one text: what changes and when the base word changes its stem, with every French example in Latin letters); `name`, `description` (ending "Not yet checked by a French speaker: please report any mistake."), `tags`, `license`, `native`.
5. **Path**: the unit's entry turns from `planned` to `decks: ["fr-grammar-<topic1>", "fr-grammar-<topic2>"]` (no `"*"` unless the unit's neighbours show one; existing grammar units end without it), `words: 0`, `grammar: [topics]` unchanged, passages and any `milestone` kept. The reading deck id (`fr-reading-<slug>`) is added by the reading task (FR-030, FR-031, FR-055 to FR-057), which edits the same entry.
6. **Reading** is a separate Haiku task (section 3.5): French grammar units, unlike the CJK ones in the build-deck skill, all write a reading deck (the 15 written ones do).
7. A grammar unit writes no card ids; no `-sentences` deck (French precedent).

Design hints per unit (Sonnet confirms against taught words and a grammar page it read; none is an asserted fact): see the rows of section 4.2.

### 3.5 Recipe RULE-READING-FR (reading decks; Haiku)

A unit's reading deck: file `decks/fr/fr-en-reading-<slug>.yaml`, deck id `fr-en-reading-<slug>`, listed on the path as `fr-reading-<slug>`; `<slug>` is the theme (word unit) or the planned id without `fr-grammar-` or `fr-` (`reflexives`, `object-pronouns`, `future`, `formal-address`). **Two passages**: the first is a short *heard* text written for the unit's listening passage description, the second a *read* text written for its reading passage description; each is `{ id: "fr-<kebab-name>", title: "<English title>", theme: "<theme id of decks/themes.yaml>" (a grammar unit uses the theme of its main words, for example `daily-life`), sentences: [4 x { text, ipa }], questions: [3 x ...] }`; `sentences` are 4 short, natural lines in French; the words are taught up to the unit (grammar units: the unit's own forms plus taught words only; no word of a still-planned unit unless merged). A `text` is kept letter for letter (typographic apostrophe policy of the existing decks: copy the nearest one). Each question: `id` (`fr-NNNN` from the range, ascending), `prompt: { en: "..." }`, and either `answer: true` or `false` (a statement) or `options: [{ en }, { en }, { en }]` (3 options) with `answer` the 1-based number of the right one; the right option is not always the first or the same number in a deck; a question answerable without the passage is not allowed. Deck `name: "Reading: <topic>"`, `description` ("Two short texts ... Written for this deck; not yet checked by a French speaker: please report any mistake."). Path edit: add `"fr-reading-<slug>"` to the unit's `decks` list **only** (word units do it in their own path edit). Not touched: every other file.

## 5. Discrepancies found while auditing (record, then decide)

1. **There is no `register` note kind.** Deck-Format "Notes" and `NOTE_KINDS` in `tools/validate_decks.py` list exactly seven: `pair`, `homograph`, `lookalike`, `culture`, `usage`, `behaviour`, `note`. `register` is a **card field** (`REGISTERS = formal, neutral, informal, slang, offensive`), required on proverb cards. Two pages say otherwise and should be corrected by their owners: [Deck-Work-Queue-ES-TE](Deck-Work-Queue-ES-TE.md) tasks ES-031 to ES-039 ("`usage`, `note`, `register`...") and RULE-NOTE of the [Deck-Work-Queue](Deck-Work-Queue.md) (Japanese). This page says seven kinds and no `register` note.
2. **The 25-word excerpt cap is not enforced for public-domain cards.** The validator limits 25 words only for `basis: "quotation"` (`QUOTATION_WORDS`, with 5 cards a deck). For `public-domain` culture cards, 25 words is this plan's own rule (taken from RULE-CULT of the Spanish and Japanese plans).
3. **Books and script.** Deck-Format says "Books are recommended only when the course includes script learning", but the validator requires every book to name a `script`, and `recommendations/es.yaml` has six books with `script: "latin"`. French follows the validator and Spanish; the owner may rule otherwise.
4. **Rule for `ref`.** The architect's CI list says a `ref` is valid only where the word equals the card target. No such check was found on the base path of `tools/validate_decks.py` (it checks `ref`/`base` exclusivity, the card id's format, that the named card exists, and that the word is one word of the text), and existing French decks use `{ word: "l'hiver", ref: "fr-1172" }` for the card `hiver`. This page keeps the stricter rule (use `ref` only for an exact match) because it is safe under both readings.
5. **Reading decks for grammar units.** The build-deck skill's CJK section says "a grammar unit keeps its planned passages and writes no reading deck"; every written French grammar unit (15 of 15) has a reading deck, and 4 of them no longer list passages. This queue follows the French precedent and owes a reading deck for each of the 25 planned grammar units.
6. **Validator floor versus French law.** The validator's `PD_TERMS["FR"] = 70` and the clock give an author-death floor of **1955** in 2026 (it moves each January). French law adds wartime and "mort pour la France" extensions the code does not know; so the builder's rule is stricter ([Plan-Culture-French](Plan-Culture-French.md)). Separately, `Plan-Culture-Spanish` states a life-plus-80 floor for Spanish authors while the validator's `ES` term is 70; not a French matter, noted for the Spanish owner.
7. **Stale comment.** The header of `decks/fr/fr-path.yaml` says "All of A1 is written; A2 and B1 are planned" and speaks of A1 "to be built after Bengali's, Hindustani's and Spanish's B1"; both are out of date (A1 and 36 further units are written). It is a comment, so nothing breaks.
8. **Issue and grid wording.** [Plan-Language-Completeness](Plan-Language-Completeness.md) section (e) says "path of 113 units, 77 built; 229 core files" for French: 113 is the number of path entries (112 units and the `*` line), and 229 is the number of YAML files directly in `decks/fr` (151 cores, 76 reading decks, `fr-facts`, `fr-path`), not of core files. The grid there lists French as "not audited"; this page is that audit.
9. **A card-id surprise.** The highest French id in use is a reading question (`fr-2804`), not a card (`fr-2788`). Any tool that computes "next id" from cards alone would reuse `fr-2789` to `fr-2804`; `validate_decks.py --next-id fr` is right (`fr-2805`).

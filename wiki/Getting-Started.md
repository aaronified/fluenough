# Getting started

## Install

Fluenough is an Android app. Download `app-release.apk` from the [latest release](https://github.com/aaronified/fluenough/releases/latest) and open it on the phone. Android asks you to allow installs from your browser or file manager: allow it. This is called sideloading.

An iPhone build is not published. To build one yourself on a Mac, see the [iOS build guide](iOS-Build-Guide).

The app comes with no decks. It downloads a language's decks the first time you choose that language. Be online for that step. After it, the app works offline.

## The first run

The first launch walks you through a few screens. You can go Back or Skip on them.

1. **Welcome.** One screen on what the app is. No account is needed.
2. **A short tour.** Slides on the four ways to practise, how words come back just before you forget them, that your progress stays on your phone, and that most languages have a fact a day.
3. **Microphone and sound check.** Speaking drills listen to you and listening drills read words aloud. You record two seconds and hear them back. Android asks to let Fluenough use the microphone. If you refuse, speaking stays off. You can switch speaking and listening on or off later, under Settings > Activities and memory. See [Speaking and listening](Speaking-and-Listening).
4. **What do you know, and what do you want to learn?** A screen explaining the pills you are about to tap: **Learn** (practise it in daily drills, its decks are downloaded), **Know** (you already can; it shapes your daily facts and the decks you can review) and **Not learning**. The icons say what you can do (read, write, hear, speak), two ticks mean a short check confirmed a level, and a touch and hold names any pill. Skip it or press Choose my languages.
5. **Your languages.** The same list as Settings > Languages, with English already known. Add a language with the search (it finds a language by its English name, its own name or another name such as Urdu); a language with more than one script, or a script of its own, first asks which script you read, in that language's own script with the English under the question. Then its skills sheet opens: tap a pill to say what you know and what you want to learn. Choose at least one language besides English to go on. Nothing is saved until the last button, and it is never sent anywhere. You can change it later in Settings, under "Languages".

## Choose what to learn

If you set a language to Learn on the previous screen, its decks start downloading when you finish and this step is skipped (find your level later from the language's sheet on Languages). Otherwise you pick the languages to learn next. They are grouped by family and you can search by name. For each one you choose, the app downloads its decks. You can start once the first five are in. The rest arrive while you learn. See [Courses and paths](Courses-and-Paths).

For a language that has an alphabet you do not read, the app asks whether to learn it in its script, or in Latin letters only. See [Scripts and readings](Scripts-and-Readings).

If a language is taught from more than one of the languages you know, you choose which to learn it from. Each teaches its own share of the course so far.

## The placement test

For each language the app asks "Do you know any of it?" If you do, a short check finds where to start.

- It asks a few words from each part of the course, until one is new to you.
- For a script it asks you to match letters, write a word in Latin letters, and write words from their meaning. If you pass, the script lessons are skipped.
- It then says where you will start, and why. You can try again from there.
- Your answers do not count as reviews, and you can still study anything later.
- The last screen asks how to review the words it placed: **Set in stone** (kept out of Today, counted as known; you can still revise them from a deck page, and those answers are not logged), **Spot-check** (a few tested now and then, and a miss brings more back; picked for you) or **Keep reviewing** (like any other word). Script decks follow your choice too. You can change it later, for each course, on the Languages page.

If you say you know none of it, you start from the beginning.

Later, if you already read a script through another language, a course offers a short test of its letters to skip its script lessons.

**What "verified" means on this page.** Every Sanskrit string below was either copied by a script from the raw wikitext of the page named in its row, or typed and then checked by the same script to occur on that page (the check found no mismatch). The pages were fetched on 2026-11-16 with `index.php?title=...&action=raw` through the sandbox proxy (the MediaWiki API answered 429 for the page-list calls and was not used for any text). Nothing was run locally (no tests, no validator, no `tools/transcribe.py`). ## Rules applied

- **Rights floor** (the Telugu page's rule, validator floor 1955): a named author must have died in 1955 or earlier; an anonymous work needs a first publication before 1900, and `public_domain_problem` reads `author_died + term` against the year. Every work below is ancient, so a named author passes by hundreds of years; the live question is the **date shape**. Culture and proverb cards are `basis: public-domain`, `jurisdiction: IN`, no `quotation` items. Anything not public domain is a recommendation (source-it-yourself): nothing of it is shipped.
- **`source.date` takes a year 1 to 9999 as a number, or a string `"c. NNN"` or `"NNN-NNN"` with three or four digits** (`parse_years` in `tools/validate_decks.py` on main, read 2026-11-16; nothing run). It takes **no BCE year**. A work whose page gives only BCE dates cannot be dated until issue #1491 / PR #1494 (BCE years in `source.date`) is merged; those rows say `needs #1494 (BCE only)`. A creator's `born` and `died` accept a negative year for BCE already, and `{ year, circa: true }`, never `circa: false`. **The spans written here as `301-500` are the page's own century bounds written as years, not a guess of a year inside them.**
- **Excerpts**: one verse, half-verse or one short prose sentence, at most 25 words, the page's own typing. Whitespace is collapsed. At most 4 cards from one work (its parts count as sub-collections), at most 3 otherwise; a story collection is the one exception: one verse for each of its books (five for the Pañcatantra, four for the Hitopadeśa) and one frame-story line. 18 per deck at most. A deck is never padded; a short deck says so.
- **Typing defects on the Sanskrit Wikisource pages** (found while reading, the flags column uses these): (1) the Hitopadeśa and the later Pañcatantra pages type `ं` for every homorganic nasal (`संधिः`, `चंद्र`), the printed text has the nasal letter; (2) an avagraha is sometimes typed as a space (`को तिभारः` for `कोऽतिभारः`); (3) `ढ` stands for `द्ध` in places (`बुढिमंतः`, `वृढ`); (4) `ॐ` stands for `ो` inside a word (`क्ॐअलं`); (5) hyphens inside compounds (`काव्य-शास्त्र-विनोदेन`) and `=` or spaces inside the Aṣṭādhyāyī lines are word-splitting marks, not part of the text; (6) the Aṣṭādhyāyī page types some sūtras wrongly (section 6). The builder copies the page, flags the defect, and never "fixes" a form from memory.
- **Reading**: IAST as the queue decided; `reading` and `ipa` come from `tools/transcribe.py sa` once SA-002 merges, never from this page. Translations are the builder's own plain renderings.
- **Creators** go in `decks/creators/sa.yaml`; `born` and `died` only where the page gives a year (section 5). For the rest the plan asks the owner, because the format needs two years.
- **Upaniṣad rows are pre-classical.** The Kena page calls itself "a Vedic Sanskrit text". The Classical Sanskrit course may carry such cards only as culture, flagged; the `vsn` session may plan the same verses, so ask the parent before building rows 5 to 11 of `sa-culture-1`.

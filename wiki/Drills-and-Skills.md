# Drills and skills

## Skills

Each word is scheduled separately in each skill. You can recognise a word long before you can write it.

| Skill | You do | Marked by |
|---|---|---|
| Seen words | See the word, recall or choose its meaning | You, or the app for a choice |
| Heard words | Hear the word, choose then type its meaning | The app |
| Spoken words | See the meaning, say the word | The app, through the phone's speech recogniser |
| Written words | See the meaning, type the word | The app |
| Grammar | See a form and choose what it means; or see a prompt and pick, then type, the right form | The app |

Reading passages come on top: read, or hear, a short passage and answer questions.

In Settings, under "Activities and memory", you can switch skills on or off. See [Profiles and settings](Profiles-and-Settings). Seen words, grammar, matching pairs and choosing answers have no switch: they are always asked, so a lesson always has questions. The drill on sound contrasts (minimal pairs) is built as a screen but is not switched on yet, so no lesson asks it. It is shown "Feature incoming" ([Roadmap](Roadmap)).

A right answer also counts in part for the skills it implies ([ADR-0034](ADR-0034-Hear-Say-Write)).

## Kinds of question

- **Choose.** Pick the right word or meaning from a few.
- **Match.** Drag a word onto its meaning, or tap one, then the other. A switch turns on hearing a word when you tap it ([ADR-0032](ADR-0032-Match-Pairs-Tap-To-Hear)).
- **Type.** Type the word in its script, or in Latin letters. The screen shows an example of the Latin spelling. If you have no keyboard for the script, a hint suggests one.
- **Put in order.** Build a sentence from word tiles.
- **Say.** Say the word. See [Speaking and listening](Speaking-and-Listening).
- **Grammar.** Pick or type the right form of a word.
- **Read.** A passage with questions.
- **Set.** A group of related words shown together in a lesson. Nothing is recorded; Continue goes on.
- **Teach.** A new word shown before you are asked about it, on one screen with no scrolling: the word large, with its reading, a speaker and its IPA; the picture beside it; the meaning; a note; and one warning. Tap the IPA for each sound of the word, from the IPA course, with its practice. Tap the meaning for the dictionary: the senses, the etymology and the examples. There is no Continue button: swipe the card, or tap the chevron that shows while you touch the screen, to go on.

## How answers are marked

When you type, the app is gentle with:

- accents and marks: "Right, but mind the accent";
- a missing article: "Right, but keep the article";
- a near miss: "Almost. Did you mean ...?" Near misses are yours to judge.

For Latin-letter answers, see [Readings and ISO 15919](Readings-ISO-15919) and [ADR-0022](ADR-0022-Romanised-Answers).

After a wrong answer, the screen shows the right one, and what you typed or said beside it. A word that sounds almost like a rude word is flagged "Careful when speaking". The flag always shows, but the rude word is named only when Adult content is on.

## Rating yourself

For some questions, such as seen words, you decide how well you knew it. Four buttons show when the word comes back: Again, Hard, Good and Easy. Pick Again if you did not know it, Easy if it was no effort.

## How words come back

Scheduling is by FSRS ([ADR-0033](ADR-0033-Fsrs)). A word you know waits longer each time. A word you miss returns sooner.

The app can fit the schedule to you, per language and skill, from your own answers. Nothing is sent anywhere ([ADR-0035](ADR-0035-Fsrs-Fitted-Per-Skill)). Until a skill has enough answers, every skill uses the same starting pace. In Settings, "Adjust to me" fits it now, and "Adjust automatically" does so by itself. A page on Progress, "Learn more about your pacing", shows how the pace changed.

Every review is written to a log, so your statistics can be recomputed ([Log format](Log-Format)).

## Cards you keep missing

A card you miss again and again is a leech. Drilling it harder rarely helps. Progress lists them. Read the card's note, then reset it to new or set it aside. See [Progress and stats](Progress-and-Stats).

## Cards and their bases

A word built on another shows its base on the card, such as "Base: जाना (jānā) · to go". A word's sheet, opened from its row on a deck's or a unit's page, shows everything the card has, unfolded: how it is said symbol by symbol, its meanings, notes, warnings, dictionary entry and examples, its tags and id, and how hard you have found it in each skill. A deck's page lists every word with its meaning and where you stand with it; its tag chips filter the words as you tap them; Practice drills the words in view in the skills you choose, and + Learn teaches the ones not taught yet.

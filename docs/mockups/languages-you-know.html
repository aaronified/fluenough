Languages you know: four ways to ask about the script

Issue #490 grows the list from 3 languages to 35. Every frame below uses one layout: a one-line search at the top, a few top picks from the phone's language and region, then all 35 languages grouped by family, each row with its English name and its own name. It is one screen, used at first launch (back, progress wave, Continue) and in Settings (app bar, back, Save); only that chrome differs. The four variations differ only in how a chosen language asks whether you read its script. Colours are Flutter's own ColorScheme.fromSeed output for the app's four seeds; the default, Clay on pure black, is the owner's phone.

The task: on a phone set to English (India), choose Hindi and Telugu from the top picks and Bhojpuri from the Indo-Aryan list (one swipe), say that you read Devanagari but not Telugu, and press Continue.

1. Inline Yes/No2. Chip with toggle3. Bottom sheet4. Speak and Read columns
Taps for 3 languages, scripts answered 7 and 1 swipe: a tap to choose and a tap to answer, each, then Continue. 7 and 2 swipes: as 1, but Bhojpuri's chip appears at the top, so you swipe back up to answer. 7 and 1 swipe: a tap opens the sheet, a tap answers, each, then Continue. 6 and 1 swipe: Speak for each, Read for Hindi and Bhojpuri; Telugu's “no” is an empty box.
Clarity High: the question appears under the row you tapped, in its own words, with a sample of the letters. Medium: the answer sits at the top, away from the row you tapped; “Reads / Speaks only” is terse. Highest: the sheet names the language, asks the one question, and offers two whole sentences. Lowest: two columns of boxes on 35 rows; “Read” for English is a strange question.
Error-proneness Low: neither answer is picked; Continue waits and says why. An unanswered row can be off-screen. Low to medium: the same waiting rule, but a chosen language from far down the list has its question out of sight. Lowest: a language cannot be chosen without an answer, so the button never waits. High: an empty Read box means both “no” and “not answered”, so the rule that nothing is assumed cannot hold.
Accessibility Good: the row grows and the new question takes focus; Yes and No are a segmented button. At 2x the buttons move under the text. Fair: focus has to jump to the top of the page; chips with a toggle inside are two nodes in one. Good: a modal sheet holds focus and returns it to the row; each choice is one 64 dp target. One extra screen change per language. Poor: each box needs “Speak Hindi” and “Read Hindi” labels; the column headings scroll away.
Recommendation
  1. 3. Bottom sheet. Choosing and answering are one act, so no language is ever left unanswered and Continue never waits for a hidden question. Main downside: a sheet opens for every language, one more screen change than inline.
  2. 1. Inline Yes/No. The same taps without a modal and the closest to today's screen, but an unanswered question can scroll out of sight.
  3. 2. Chip with toggle, then 4. Speak and Read columns, which cannot tell “no” from “not answered”.

Swipe the top row to change language

#528. On Today, Decks and Progress, a sideways swipe on the top row moves to the next or previous language. The top row is the header row the language menu already sits in. Only that row takes the swipe (owner, 2026-10-16: “Swipe on top row only”). The rest of the screen keeps its own scrolling, the sideways chip rows included. The row shows which language is active: the menu's glyph, as today, and a row of marks under it, one per choice. The swipe changes the same choice the menu does (#461), so all three screens follow it. Hindi and Bengali throughout, and Telugu and Marathi for four languages. Colours are Flutter's own ColorScheme.fromSeed output for the app's four seeds. The dashed outlines mark what changes, and the grey finger and its trail show the gesture. Neither is part of the app. Anything marked Proposed is not decided yet and is listed in the decisions table at the end.

1 · Today, with two languages: before, during and after

The learner learns Hindi and Bengali. The menu offers All languages, Hindi and Bengali, so the row has three marks, in the menu's order. Hindi is shown. Swiping left (the finger moving from right to left) goes to the next choice, Bengali. Swiping right goes back, to Hindi, then to All languages.

  • Proposed While dragging, the active mark stretches toward the next one, the menu's glyph slides with the finger, and a label under the row names the language a release will show. Nothing else on the screen moves.
  • Proposed Release: the change happens past a third of the row's width, or on a quick flick. Short of that, the row springs back and nothing changes.
  • Proposed After: the screen below shows the new language at once (a short cross-fade). The label stays for about a second and fades out, and a screen reader says “Language shown: Bengali”.
  • Proposed The label floats over the top of the screen like a tooltip. It takes no taps, and it covers what is under it only while it shows.

2 · Decks and Progress, with four languages

The learner learns Hindi, Bengali, Telugu and Marathi: five marks with All languages. On Decks the path opens at the new course's unit up next, as it does when the menu changes it today. On Progress, All languages still has its language chips under the row. Those chips scroll sideways by themselves, and the swipe belongs to the top row only.

  • Proposed No wrap-around. Past the last language (Marathi) or before All languages, the row moves a little with the finger and springs back. A swipe never jumps from the end to the start.

3 · 1.4x text

At 1.4x, the app's cap, the row is taller but works the same. The marks and the glyph tile don't grow with the text, like the glyph tiles today. The label grows with the text.

4 · Taps, and the screen reader

The swipe is a shortcut and replaces nothing. A tap on the menu button opens the menu as today, and choosing a language there moves the marks too. With TalkBack on, a one-finger swipe moves TalkBack's focus and doesn't reach the app. So the menu button offers two actions, “Next language” and “Previous language”, in TalkBack's actions menu, beside double-tap to open the menu.

  • Proposed The marks are hidden from screen readers. The menu button already says which language is shown (“Language shown: Hindi”).
  • Proposed The actions are named with the language they lead to: “Next language: Bengali” and “Previous language: All languages”. At an end, the action that would go past it isn't offered.
  • Proposed With reduce motion on (Android's “Remove animations”), the change is instant: no stretching mark, no slide, no fade. The label still shows for a second.

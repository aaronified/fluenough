# ADR-0025: Readings use ISO 15919 letters as the word is said, with the IPA beside them

- **Status:** Accepted. Supersedes ADR-0022 where it chose one popular,
  unmarked scheme per language; how typed answers are graded stands.
- **Date:** 2026-10-04

> **Note, 2026-10-09.** The readings are *based on* ISO 15919's letters, not
> a strict ISO 15919 transliteration. The owner: "the docs should not say
> that ISO15919 is strictly followed, since we have made changes in it
> whenever we judged deviations made sense from a learner perspective."
> The deliberate deviations (inherent a, anusvara, Bengali's and Assamese's
> ô, Assamese x, merged long vowels and others) are listed with an example
> and the reason for each in the
> [README](../../README.md#where-the-readings-depart-from-iso-15919). Where
> this record says "ISO 15919's letters", read "letters based on ISO 15919's".
> The decision text below is left as it was written.

## Context

ADR-0022 wrote each language's readings in the spelling people type in
chat: lowercase ASCII, no length or retroflex marks. The owner, on the
first card of the first Telugu deck, which showed పాలు as *palu*: "We
need a stricter short/long and weird pronounciations system than just
using the normal one. There is no way to visually differentiate palu from
palu, and this is the very first deck." The deck that teaches the
difference, "Sounds to tell apart", wrote both words *palu* and both
words for old and song *pata*.

The owner asked for an app-wide change, "palu, paalu type change", and
for "the IPA standarised versions for all words". Asked how, they
decided:

- the letters are ISO 15919's, spelled out in the README, and this is
  not for typed answers, "which will accept all variations";
- where the script and the sound differ, follow the sound: "Follow IPA".
  ISO 15919 letters, spelled as the word is said;
- a typed answer with the wrong length or retroflex is right, as before;
- the IPA shows on the teach card, after an answer, in Inspect, and on
  questions too, unless the question is about pronunciation;
- the IPA is broad: /paːlu/.

## Decision

- **Readings** of the Indic languages are ISO 15919's letters, written as
  the word is said. ā ī ū ē ō mark length where the language says it
  long. ṭ ḍ ṇ ḷ mark the retroflex consonants and ś ṣ ṅ ñ the others.
  Two adjustments follow the sound. A letter not said is left out (Hindi
  *kitnā*). A letter said as another is written as that one: Bengali ঈ is
  *i* and Assamese স is *x*. Bengali's and Assamese's own vowel is *ô*,
  the letter ISO 15919 gives ऑ, and a nasal vowel ends in *m̐*. Each
  language's romanisation file says how, in a paragraph, and names the
  standard (`standard: "ISO 15919"`). The README has the table.
- **IPA**: every card, grammar cell, passage sentence, glossary word and
  script term has an `ipa`. It is broad: phonemes, length, aspiration,
  nasal vowels, Spanish stress. It is written without slashes, which the
  app adds. Spanish cards have one too, as the decks' es-ES voice says
  them.
- **Typed answers** are graded as leniently as before. Each romanisation
  file lists how its letters are typed (`typed`: c as ch, ś as sh, m̐ as
  n). A reading is spelled that way before an answer is compared with it,
  and the grader folds ISO 15919's marks away. A test checks that the old
  readings, typed, are still exact against the new ones.
- **The tool**: `tools/transcribe.py` writes a reading and IPA for a word.
  It reads the word's letters against a hint, the word as people type
  it, which shows what is said. Where the hint does not fit, it uses
  rules, and says so. The decks were converted with it, using each old
  reading as the hint. The readings quoted in notes changed with their
  words.

## Consequences

- All of the decks' 8,229 readings were rewritten. Of the old readings, 31
  no longer match their new reading exactly when typed. They are letter cards of conjuncts, old
  spellings, and a few words typed with a sound the letters lack (Hindi
  *khatam* for ख़त्म). They grade as near misses, which the learner
  judges, not as wrong.
- The IPA is generated by rule from the readings, and no speaker has
  checked it yet. Known limits: Bengali এ said [æ] is written e unless
  the old reading said so; Marathi's ts/dz before back vowels follows the
  common rule, which loanwords break; Telugu's [ts] is written /tʃ/,
  broad. The decks are tagged unreviewed, and reports are welcome.
- ISO 15919 marks need a font that has them. Android's Roboto and Noto
  do.
- Readings are longer to read for a learner who never wanted the marks.
  Typing them is never required.

## Alternatives considered

- **Doubled vowels and capitals (paalu, paaTa)**, as in ITRANS. These
  are plain ASCII, but the capitals look odd. The owner chose ISO 15919.
- **Dots under, doubled vowels (paaṭa)**: half of one standard and half
  of another.
- **Strict ISO 15919, letter for letter** (*kitanā*). This is reversible
  and exact, but suggests sounds that are not said. The owner chose to
  follow the sound.
- **The IPA only, readings unchanged**: it leaves the reading, which every
  card shows, still unable to tell pālu from palu.
- **IPA computed in the app from the script**: per-language phonology in
  Dart, against "a language is data". The data carries the IPA instead,
  and the tool lives with the deck tools.
